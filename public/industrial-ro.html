Engineering Baseline

Why Basis-of-Design Documentation Matters

In industrial procurement, a generic equipment datasheet is not an engineering specification. Reverse osmosis performance depends strictly on feed water chemistry, seasonal temperature shifts, silt density index (SDI), osmotic pressure barriers, and downstream hydraulic demand. When equipment is installed without rigorous design documentation, membrane foulants quickly overwhelm the skid, leading to premature scaling, loss of permeate flux, and shortened membrane life.

Tovila delivers every industrial RO skid backed by complete basis-of-design documentation. This engineering record outlines raw water projections, scaling indices, specific membrane element flux rates, array staging ratios, and interstage pressure balances. It provides plant engineers with an empirical operational benchmark rather than speculative vendor claims.

The Technical Deliverable

The Basis-of-Design Package

A comprehensive engineering record detailing raw feed water parameters, membrane projection models, differential pressure baseline limits, and hydraulic recovery thresholds. It serves as the permanent reference for performance audits, warranty validation, and scheduled maintenance.

Note: Membrane configuration, vessel array stages, feed pressures, and recovery targets are established during the site-specific engineering assessment based on laboratory water analysis, not assumed standard values.

Membrane Architecture

Four Pillars of Industrial RO Design

Engineered around raw water chemistry, membrane longevity, and reliable process feed.

Capacity-Matched Membrane Trains

Membrane arrays sized directly against measured continuous and peak production demand. Rather than deploying oversized single units or strained under-capacity skids, staging is balanced to maintain stable element flux and minimize concentration polarization across vessels.

  • Multi-stage vessel arrangements balancing hydraulic flux
  • Variable-frequency drive high-pressure booster pumps
  • Automated concentrate throttling for stable recovery control

Defined Recovery & Rejection Targets

Recovery rates and ion rejection thresholds are modeled against raw water scaling tendencies and mineral solubility limits. Operating targets are specified to prevent silica precipitation, calcium sulfate scaling, and biological fouling across all operating seasons.

  • Conservative membrane element flux rates prevent rapid compaction
  • Targeted ion rejection tailored to downstream process requirements
  • Continuous conductivity and temperature tracking at permeate discharge

Integrated Clean-in-Place (CIP) Regimes

Dedicated CIP skid integration with chemical circulation heating, multi-stage recirculation loops, and low-pressure flushing protocols. Scheduled cleaning regimes restore transmembrane flux before irreversible foulant compaction damages active polyamide layers.

  • Automated low-pressure permeate flush cycles on shutdown
  • Dual chemical batching tanks for acid and alkaline wash sequences
  • Differential pressure tracking across vessel stages triggers cleaning intervals

Commissioning & Handover Records

Prior to plant handover, our engineers execute rigorous commissioning validation. Baseline differential pressures, salt passage rates, normalized permeate flows, and emergency interlocks are formally documented to establish the site's ongoing operational baseline.

  • Initial baseline normalization logs for long-term trend analysis
  • Instrument calibration certificates and sensor verification records
  • Structured operator training and preventative maintenance manuals
Technical Governance

Empirical Testing Over Vendor Assertions

Every industrial reverse osmosis proposal from Tovila originates with comprehensive feed water characterization. Silt density index (SDI), total dissolved solids, dissolved silica, heavy metals, and biofouling risks are evaluated before membrane element selection or pump curves are finalized.

Site Specifics

Final recovery rates, membrane vessel quantity, high-pressure pump kilowatts, and pre-treatment filtration stages are confirmed during the engineering assessment for that site.

Questions & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What documentation do we receive?

Clients receive full basis-of-design documentation, piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs), membrane projection model outputs, electrical schematics, instrument calibration records, and formal commissioning test logs. The final scope of documentation is confirmed per project contract.

How is system capacity determined?

System capacity is matched directly to measured continuous process demand, peak instantaneous draw, and storage buffering requirements determined during the initial engineering assessment, rather than selected from off-the-shelf standard sizes.
