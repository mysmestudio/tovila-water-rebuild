Advanced Engineering Discipline

Moving Beyond Routine Operation

Standard operators respond to alarms after membranes fail or flows decline. Advanced engineers model thermodynamic shifts, normalize empirical data, dissect failed elements down to microscopic foulants, and program deterministic control logic to prevent degradation before it begins.

Discipline 01

Performance Normalisation

Adjusting readings for temperature and pressure to compare system performance fairly over time.

Raw operational data is inherently deceptive. If feedwater cools from 31°C in April to 24°C in July, permeate flow naturally drops by roughly 20% purely due to increased water viscosity — not because the membrane is fouled.

You learn to apply ASTM D4516 standard algorithms: calculating the Temperature Correction Factor (TCF), Net Driving Pressure (NDP), and osmotic pressure gradients. You separate genuine fouling from baseline environmental shifts to schedule cleanings on mathematical necessity rather than guesswork.

Discipline 02

Membrane Autopsy Interpretation

Diagnosing why a membrane failed from its physical and chemical post-mortem condition.

When an element suffers irreversible loss of flux or high salt passage, forensic autopsy reveals the root operational cause. You learn how to perform non-destructive and destructive element examinations:

  • Weight differential checking for inorganic scale accumulation.
  • Fiberglass outer wrap cutting and spiral unrolling technique.
  • Feed spacer inspection for bio-slime, silt, and iron sedimentation.
  • Loss on Ignition (LOI) to differentiate organic bio-fouling from inorganic minerals.
  • Fujiwara and dye testing to detect permanent polyamide degradation from halogen/chlorine exposure.
Discipline 03

Automation & Optimisation

Tuning and automating a running system rather than merely operating it.

Learn how to calibrate closed-loop controls on industrial PLC platforms. Program PID control loops for variable frequency drives (VFD) to maintain constant permeate production under variable feed pressures.

Configure differential pressure (ΔP) automated flush cycles, integrate oxidation-reduction potential (ORP) emergency trip interlocks to protect membranes from upstream chemical dosing overruns, and calculate specific energy consumption ($kWh/m^3$) to optimize operating expenditure.

Advanced Curriculum Modules

Analytical & Diagnostic Modules

Rigorous technical coursework structured for practicing utility engineers, plant superintendents, and senior water treatment specialists.

Module A

Membrane Transport Physics & Projection Modeling

Review solution-diffusion transport equations, solvent and solute permeability coefficients (A and B values), concentration polarization at the membrane boundary layer, and osmotic pressure calculation via Van ’t Hoff approximations. Model array staging (2:1 or 3:2 arrays) using commercial projection software against varying Ghanaian borehole water chemistries.

Module B

Scaling Kinetics & Antiscalant Optimisation

Analyze scaling kinetics for calcium carbonate, barium sulfate, calcium sulfate, and reactive vs. non-reactive silica ($SiO_2$). Evaluate antiscalant threshold inhibition, crystal distortion mechanisms, and dispersant characteristics to safely optimize system recovery by balancing water efficiency against scaling risk without precipitating mineral foulants.

Module C

Forensic Autopsy Laboratory Protocols

Conduct hands-on autopsies on retired industrial elements. Perform acid and caustic solubility tests to confirm iron hydroxide ($Fe(OH)_3$), calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$), or biofilm matrices. Document formal autopsy reports specifying corrective actions for upstream plant operation.

Module D

Telemetry, SCADA & Energy Minimisation

Instrumenting membrane arrays for remote SCADA telemetry. Setting up automated data normalization feeds in industrial historians, programming high/low differential pressure warnings, implementing energy recovery turbines/exchangers, and reducing electrical operating costs per cubic metre.

Engineer Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Specific guidelines for prospective Advanced RO participants.

Who is this suitable for?

This programme is designed explicitly for experienced operators, plant engineers, utility managers, and industrial chemical engineers who already possess foundational knowledge of reverse osmosis mechanics and are actively managing operational water systems. This is not an entry-level track; candidates without prior water treatment experience should consider the Online Courses or Hands-On RO Training.

Does this connect to Tovila’s industrial engineering work?

Yes, directly. The coursework covers the exact automation architectures, safety interlocks, and optimisation principles implemented on Tovila’s turnkey industrial treatment plants and campus installations across Ghana. Case studies examine real anonymized operating data from manufacturing plants, food & beverage processors, and institutions engineered by Tovila.
