Pre-Treatment for Industrial Membrane Systems

Reverse osmosis membranes are precision molecular separation barriers, not filters for gross particulate matter. Every dollar invested in high-efficiency pre-treatment protects ten dollars in capital membrane assets and chemical cleaning downtime.

In membrane plant design, the adage is absolute: The success of a reverse osmosis plant is 90% determined before water ever reaches the high-pressure pump. If pre-treatment fails, the RO skid will experience chronic flux decline, premature element replacement, and high operating costs regardless of membrane brand.

The Multi-Barrier Pre-Treatment Architecture

Depending on raw feed source (surface river, coastal borehole, or industrial effluent), industrial pre-treatment utilizes a synchronized combination of unit processes:

Barrier 01

Coagulation & Flocculation

Destabilizes colloidal clay, silica sols, and humic macromolecules using Polyaluminium Chloride (PAC) or ferric salts, followed by gentle flocculation to form settleable pin-floc.

Barrier 02

Granular Depth Filtration

Pressurized multi-media filters (anthracite, quartz sand, and garnet gravel) providing true deep-bed particle capture down to 10–15 microns with air-scour backwash cycles.

Barrier 03

Catalytic Iron & Manganese Removal

Chemical pre-oxidation followed by catalytic media filtration (Greensand Plus or manganese dioxide) to drive dissolved Fe and Mn below 0.05 mg/L before membrane contact.

Barrier 04

Total Dechlorination

Thin-film composite (TFC) polyamide membranes have near-zero chlorine tolerance. Granular Activated Carbon (GAC) beds or Sodium Metabisulfite (SMBS) dosing eliminates all free halogens.

Critical Pre-Treatment Threshold Parameters

The engineer must design pre-treatment systems to guarantee that feed water entering the RO skid meets these strict manufacturer warranties:

Feed Water Quality Limits for Polyamide RO Elements

  • Silt Density Index (SDI15): < 3.0 (Maximum allowable: 5.0)
  • Turbidity: < 0.2 NTU (Maximum allowable: 1.0 NTU)
  • Free Chlorine (Cl2): < 0.1 mg/L (Continuous exposure limit < 0.02 mg/L; max tolerance 1,000 ppm-hours)
  • Total Dissolved Iron (Fe): < 0.05 mg/L
  • Total Manganese (Mn): < 0.02 mg/L
  • Oil and Grease: 0.0 mg/L (Undetectable; hydrocarbons irreversibly coat polyamide sheets)
  • Oxidation-Reduction Potential (ORP): < 250 – 300 mV (confirms total absence of oxidizing halogens)

Dechlorination Chemistry: GAC vs. Sodium Metabisulfite (SMBS)

Free chlorine oxidizes the amide nitrogen bonds in polyamide membranes, permanently breaking cross-linked polymers and destroying salt rejection. Two methods are used to achieve complete dechlorination:

Option A: Granular Activated Carbon (GAC) Beds

GAC removes chlorine through catalytic reduction (adsorbing hypochlorous acid and releasing chloride ions and carbon oxides). It requires no daily chemical preparation and provides organic adsorption. However, warm GAC beds in tropical climates like Ghana become breeding grounds for bacteria, transforming an inorganic chlorine problem into a severe downstream biofouling problem unless routinely steamed or sanitized.

Option B: Chemical Sodium Metabisulfite (SMBS) Dosing

In industrial facilities, precision metering of Sodium Metabisulfite (Na2S2O5) is the industry benchmark:

Dechlorination Stoichiometry

Na2S2O5 + H2O → 2 NaHSO3
2 NaHSO3 + 2 Cl2 + 2 H2O → Na2SO4 + 4 HCl + H2SO4

Stoichiometrically, 1.34 mg of SMBS neutralizes 1.0 mg of free chlorine. In practice, engineers dose 1.8 to 2.0 mg per mg of chlorine to maintain a safe 100% stoichiometric excess.

Advanced Pre-Treatment: Ultrafiltration (UF) Membranes

For surface water intakes (rivers, reservoirs, open lagoons) where raw water turbidity spikes erratically during rainy seasons, conventional media filters frequently experience breakthrough, causing catastrophic RO fouling.

Tovila engineers recommend hollow-fiber Ultrafiltration (UF) membranes (0.02 micron pore size) as direct pretreatment. Pressurized or submerged UF modules act as an absolute physical barrier against suspended silt, colloidal silica, bacteria, and cysts — consistently delivering feed water with an SDI15 < 1.5 and turbidity < 0.05 NTU regardless of raw river turbidity.
