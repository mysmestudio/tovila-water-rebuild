Is RO Necessary for Drinking Water?

Reverse Osmosis (RO) provides the most thorough filtration physically possible for household drinking water. But does every home in Ghana actually need one? Here is an honest engineering breakdown of what RO does, when it is truly essential, and when simpler filtration wins.

In retail water filtration, Reverse Osmosis is often marketed as the default “gold standard” that every family must install. While RO is undeniably the most powerful separation technology available for domestic use, installing it without understanding your source water leads to unnecessary water waste and maintenance costs.

What Reverse Osmosis Actually Does

Standard water filters — like spun sediment polypropylene or activated carbon blocks — trap physical particles and adsorb chemical odours through pores sized between 1 and 20 microns (0.001 to 0.02 millimetres).

A Reverse Osmosis semi-permeable membrane, by contrast, operates at a molecular scale. Its microscopic pore diameter is approximately 0.0001 microns — small enough to reject dissolved mineral ions, heavy metal atoms, and organic molecules. Water is forced across this membrane under booster pump pressure, leaving behind up to 98% of all dissolved solids.

What RO Removes That Standard Filters Cannot:

  • Dissolved Inorganic Salts: Sodium, chloride, brackish salinity, sulfates.
  • Heavy Metals: Lead, arsenic, mercury, hexavalent chromium.
  • Hazardous Inorganics: Nitrates and nitrites (from agricultural fertilizer run-off or nearby septic leaching), fluoride.
  • Micro-pollutants: Pesticides, dissolved pharmaceuticals, industrial solvent traces.
  • Microorganisms: Viruses, bacteria, and protozoan cysts (cryptosporidium/giardia).

When RO Is Genuinely Necessary

In Ghana, we recommend installing a dedicated under-sink or point-of-use drinking RO system when the raw water exhibits any of the following characteristics:

  • High Total Dissolved Solids (TDS > 500–1,000 mg/L): Common in coastal boreholes (e.g. Kokrobite, Bortianor, Prampram, Sakumono, Ada), where seawater intrusion gives the water a salty, brackish taste. No amount of carbon or sand filtering can remove dissolved salt — only membrane separation (RO) works.
  • High Nitrate Levels: Frequent in densely populated urban communities where boreholes sit within 15–30 metres of soakaways or pit latrines. Nitrates pose a serious health hazard to infants (“blue baby syndrome”) and pregnant mothers. Standard carbon filters have 0% nitrate removal; RO removes up to 95%.
  • Heavy Metal and Mining Runoff Concerns: Boreholes located downstream of artisanal gold mining areas (galamsey) or industrial zones where arsenic, mercury, or lead could contaminate the water table.
  • Fluoride Contamination: Naturally elevated fluoride in parts of Northern Ghana and the Upper East/West regions causing dental and skeletal fluorosis. RO reliably rejects excess fluoride.

When RO Is NOT Necessary

If your water comes from a reliable municipal Ghana Water Company Limited (GWCL) connection or a clean inland freshwater borehole with low dissolved solids (TDS < 200 mg/L), an RO system is often over-engineered.

In these situations, a high-grade multi-stage microfiltration or ultrafiltration system — combining a 5-micron sediment pre-filter, a high-iodine activated carbon block for chlorine/odour/volatile organics, and an Ultraviolet (UV) steriliser — will provide 100% microbiologically safe, crisp, clean drinking water without the drawbacks of RO.

The Two Trade-Offs You Must Know

1. Concentrate (Reject) Water

To keep the RO membrane from instantly scaling up, the system constantly flushes a stream of brine (reject water) down the drain. Depending on membrane efficiency and pressure, domestic systems produce between 1 to 3 litres of reject water for every 1 litre of pure water produced.

2. Demineralization & Taste

Because RO rejects all minerals indiscriminately, it strips natural calcium and magnesium. Highly demineralized water can taste “flat” or acidic. High-end Tovila RO systems include a final alkaline remineralisation cartridge to reintroduce healthy trace minerals and balance pH.

Decision Framework: RO vs. Standard Filtration

How to Choose:

Step 1: Test Your TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)

  • TDS < 250 mg/L & No Chemical Toxins: Choose a 3-Stage Carbon/Sediment/UV system. Simpler to maintain, zero water waste, natural mineral taste.
  • TDS 250 – 500 mg/L: Choose based on taste and specific contaminants (e.g. nitrates, chlorine by-products).
  • TDS > 500 mg/L or Brackish / Salty: Choose Reverse Osmosis with post-remineralisation. It is the only physical technology that will deliver healthy, refreshing drinking water.

Summary

Don’t buy an RO system simply because a salesperson told you it is “the best.” Buy an RO system because a water test proved that your source contains dissolved salts, nitrates, or chemical contaminants that standard physical filtration cannot capture.
